# Iron Gambit — 90-Day Launch Plan

**Position:** Heavyweight Houston apparel built on one idea — a gambit is a calculated sacrifice. You give something up to take position. That's the trade, and the people who wear it are already making it.

**The one metric that matters:** drop-list signups before Drop 01. Everything below feeds that number.
Target: **1,000 emails/SMS before you take a single order.** At a realistic 12–18% first-drop conversion, that's 120–180 units. Your first drop is 240 units. So 1,000 is the floor, not the goal.

**Second metric:** sell-through on Drop 01. Not revenue. If you sell 240/240, you priced too low or made too few — and either way you now have proof. If you sell 90, your problem is upstream in the list.

---

## The strategic call: inventory before audience is how apparel brands die

You have a name and a logo. The instinct is to order 240 units and then figure out selling. Don't.

Run **Phase 1 with zero inventory** except samples. Build the list, prove demand, *then* place the production order. If you can't get 1,000 people on a list, you definitely can't move 240 units, and you'll have found that out for the cost of some sample tees instead of $8K in blanks.

---

## Phase 1 — Days 1–30: Build the list, sell nothing

**Goal: 1,000 drop-list signups. Zero product for sale.**

Scarcity you haven't earned is just a website. Scarcity people watched you build is a brand.

### Week 1 — Foundation
- Ship the site. Wire the email form to Klaviyo (free under 250 contacts, best-in-class for apparel flows).
- Lock IG + TikTok handles. Same handle both places, even if you have to compromise.
- Order **samples only** — 3–5 pieces across your four SKUs. You need something to photograph and something to hand people.
- Set up the Klaviyo welcome flow: 3 emails over 10 days. (1) What a gambit is and why the brand is called this. (2) The fabric decision — show the weight spec, show a competitor's 4.2oz next to your 8oz. (3) Where you'll be in person.

### Weeks 2–4 — The build-in-public run
This is the whole play. You're not marketing a product, you're documenting a founding.

**Post 5x/week minimum, both platforms.** Content pillars:

| Pillar | What it looks like | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **The build** | Sample boxes opening, rejected prints, fabric weight comparisons, the pantone that came back wrong | Highest-performing content for pre-launch apparel, by a mile. People invest in the making. |
| **The philosophy** | 20-second reads on the gambit idea — what you gave up to start this, framed as strategy not sacrifice-porn | This is your moat. Any brand can print a tee. This is why yours means something. |
| **Chess as content** | Famous gambits explained in 30 seconds, tactics puzzles, chess-in-Houston culture | Free reach. Chess content overperforms wildly on TikTok and it's genuinely on-brand for you. |
| **Houston** | Where you shoot, where you print, the city as backdrop | Local audience converts to in-person sales, which is your best margin. |

**The single highest-leverage video you can make:** the fabric weight test. Two tees, yours and a fast-fashion one, held up to light. No talking. Text on screen. This format sells apparel because it's a demonstration, not a claim.

**Metric checkpoint at day 30:** if you're under 400 signups, stop and diagnose before ordering inventory. The problem is almost always the content, not the audience size.

## Phase 2 — Days 31–60: Get physical, then produce

**Goal: 3 in-person events, 2,000 total signups, production order placed.**

### In-person (your unfair advantage)
Houston has a real market circuit and most online-first brands ignore it. You shouldn't — table sales carry ~2x the margin of a shipped order and every conversation is a permanent customer.

**Targets:**
- **HTown Market at POST Houston** — large recurring vendor market downtown, heavy foot traffic, the right demographic. Apply via POST's vendor form. This is your priority booking.
- **Pop Up Collective** — a permanent Houston shop that runs vendor markets twice monthly and rents individual brand space. The cheapest route to a physical shelf; get on the calendar.
- **Search Eventbrite Houston weekly** for "vendor market" and "pop up shop." Booth fees run roughly $50–$100 for a 10x10. Budget for 3.
- **Non-obvious venues that fit this brand specifically:** chess clubs and tournaments (Houston Chess Club, scholastic tournaments — parents buy), boxing/MMA gyms, car meets, barbershops. A rack at a barbershop on consignment costs you nothing and puts the brand in front of the same 40 guys every week.

**Table strategy:** don't try to sell. Get emails. Sign, QR code, "join the list, get 24 hours early access." Sell samples if people insist. You're farming the list at higher conversion than any ad will ever give you.

### Production
Place the order once you clear **1,200 signups**, not before. Size the run to 20% of list, floor of 200 units.

### Collabs
Line up 5–8 Houston micro-creators (5K–50K followers — higher engagement, will actually respond, cheap or free). Chess players, streetwear accounts, local photographers, gym owners. Offer product, not cash. Ask for one post and one story, and get their frames for your own feed.

## Phase 3 — Days 61–90: Drop 01

**Goal: 70%+ sell-through in the first week.**

### The 10-day run-up
| Day | Move |
|---|---|
| −10 | Announce the date. Countdown goes live on site. |
| −7 | Lookbook drops. Full campaign frames, all four pieces. |
| −5 | Behind-the-scenes of the shoot. |
| −3 | Individual product reveals — one per day, one per SKU. |
| −1 | "Tomorrow." Story countdown sticker. Email #1. |
| 0 | **Early access opens for drop list only, 24 hours.** Email + SMS. |
| +1 | Public release. Post the sell-through numbers live — "IG-02 is gone." Real scarcity is the best ad you'll ever run. |
| +3 | Restock-never reminder. Push remaining sizes. |
| +7 | Close. Post final numbers. Thank the list by name. |

### After the drop
- Every buyer gets a packing insert with a QR to a review/photo submission. UGC is your Drop 02 content budget.
- Post the sell-through publicly regardless of outcome. Transparency compounds.
- Open Drop 02 waitlist within 48 hours while attention is hot.

## Things that will go wrong, and what to do

- **Signups stall around 300.** Your content is about the product, not the idea. Shift the ratio toward the philosophy and chess pillars.
- **Great engagement, no signups.** No reason to join. Add a drop-list-exclusive colorway.
- **Market table gets no traffic.** Wrong market. Craft/mom markets won't buy $88 hoodies. Get to car meets, fight cards, chess tournaments.
- **You sell out in an hour.** Made too few, or priced too low. Raise Drop 02 by $8–10 and double the run.
- **You sell 30%.** Fabric story didn't land or the price/quality gap isn't visible in photos. Reshoot showing detail — seams, weight, collar.
